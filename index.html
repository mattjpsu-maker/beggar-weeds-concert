Event Overview

Join us for a conversation with the band Beggar Weeds to explore succeeding in the music industry; songwriting and the creative process; the role of underground musical scenes in forming identity and community; and how the DIY ethos of alternative music fits within the global music industry.

Formed in Jacksonville, Florida, in the mid-1980s, Beggar Weeds emerged from an underground scene and participated in a nationwide do-it-yourself network sustained by all ages clubs, underground magazines, self-released recordings, and touring circuits that operated largely outside the major-label system.

Drawing on post-punk, Southern folk, and alternative rock traditions, Beggar Weeds released the 1988 EP Sure Pants Alot. Michael Stipe of R.E.M. later co-produced additional sessions whose recordings now appear on the 2026 career retrospective Tragedy in U.S. History. The group’s history provides a case study in regional cultural production, independent distribution, artistic collaboration, and the changing relationship between artists and audiences in a digital age.

The event will feature archival recordings and a live performance.

In the late 1980s, the whole of the Southern alternative scene knew Beggar Weeds as the up-and-coming speed-folk outfit co-signed by Michael Stipe of R.E.M. fame and destined for an imminent break into the big time. They spent most of their time shredding at the legendary venue Einstein A Go-Go and hightailing it from the near-constant fights in Jacksonville’s “blighted area” as fast as their wingtips could take them. Nearly 40 years after the release of their first and only EP, Sure Pants Alot, Beggar Weeds has somehow found themselves poised to take back the spotlight that so cruelly evaded them by barely an inch the first time around.

 

 

The CADETS

 

As the Cadets began rumbling away in 1995, Cash Carter built his frenetic, thundering drum style around Chuck Smyth’s fast-twitch searing lead rhythms and melodies fertile with nervous energy. Inside those urgent drums and angular chords came their melodic hooks, catchy and strong, laden with unexpected twists. They released a 7”,The Search for the Edible Man (1997), and their first full-length record, The Cadets (1999).

 

As the century drew to a close, the Cadets took on bassist Chad Matheny (Emperor X), a dynamo of creativity and sonic agitation. Also joining was Isaac Bear, brilliantly color washing in the open spaces with expertly placed keys and guitars. The result was a delicately balanced collage of daydreams and found sounds dangling on the hilt of a steel edge. This lineup toured Japan and all along the US east coast until 2007.

 

The Cadets’ music continues to be delightfully peculiar and full of immediacy, arriving at your ear having skidded briskly across the table yet perfectly upright and nary a drop wasted. Their sonic experiments produce songs that possess a rare energy that conjures the early days of rock & roll, sci-fi adventures, and diary entries never intended for the light of day. Lyrics filter larger themes through a tilted and tinted lens of wide-eyed fantastic reverie. Brainwashed spies, mythical chimera, desperate refugees, and jilted lovers all become protagonists and truth tellers.

 

The Cadets will release an album on Bar None in early October.
